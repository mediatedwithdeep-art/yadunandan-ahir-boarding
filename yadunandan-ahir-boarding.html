The campus

Distractions stay outside the gate.

Rajkot has no shortage of ways to lose a year. This campus is built so that the easy thing to do is also the right thing to do — study early, eat properly, play hard in the evening, sleep on time.

You live among boys from your own community who are chasing the same exams and the same standards. That brotherhood does more for discipline than any rule on a notice board. Seniors pull juniors up, the warden knows every name, and nobody quietly disappears for three weeks.

It's a simple trade: fewer choices, better habits — and years the family doesn't have to worry about.

  • DisciplineFixed timings for study, meals, ground and lights out — supervised, not assumed.
  • BrotherhoodA senior–junior culture where results and conduct are everyone's business.
  • FocusA silent reading hall and phone-down study hours during exam season.

Before you call

Questions parents ask first.

Who can apply?

Ahir students studying in Rajkot — school, college, or preparing for competitive exams. Call us with the student's course and year and we'll tell you straight away whether a seat is available.

How is admission confirmed?

Visit the campus with the student, meet the warden, and see the rooms, kitchen and grounds yourself. If it's the right fit on both sides, the form and fee are completed the same day.

What is the food like?

Pure vegetarian Gujarati meals — three times a day, on a fixed menu rotation, cooked fresh on campus. Parents are welcome to inspect the kitchen during a visit.

Can parents visit and stay in touch?

Yes. Parents can visit during visiting hours, and the warden is reachable on phone for attendance, health or discipline matters at any time.

Are seats available all year?

Seats are limited and fill fastest before the academic year begins. Call to check current availability rather than assuming — it changes month to month.
